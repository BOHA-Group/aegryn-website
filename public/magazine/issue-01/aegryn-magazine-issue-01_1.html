Built to be acquired.
Not just to exist.

There are two kinds of tech companies: those built to operate, and those built to transact. The distinction is not visible from the outside. It is entirely in the choices made before revenue was meaningful — before any buyer ever appeared.

The companies that transact well are almost always the ones where the founder, consciously or not, imposed a standard on the operation early: clean books, owned IP, documented architecture, transferable client relationships.

The companies that transact poorly are the ones where none of those choices were made deliberately — and where the cost of that informality materialises, in full, during the 14 weeks of due diligence.

The baseline illusion

Most founders estimate their valuation by applying a multiple to their ARR. That number is the ceiling, not the floor. Between the number in a founder's head and the number in a signed LOI lie four systematic adjustments that every serious buyer applies — without exception, and usually without announcing them.

None of these adjustments require exotic financial modelling. All of them are based on observable, correctable characteristics of the asset. The distance between knowing this at year two versus year six is often measured in hundreds of thousands of euros.

Founders who arrive prepared close 38% faster and command a premium of 28% above comparable uncertified assets. The preparation is entirely on the seller's clock.

Build — Vibe Coding17

The founder who built 70% of his product with Cursor in six months is not stupid. He is rational. The economics of AI-assisted development are compelling. The problem is not the tooling. The problem is what the tooling leaves behind when the technical auditor opens the repository.

The auditor is not impressed by speed. The auditor is counting: test coverage percentage, documentation density, dependency freshness, presence of deprecated API calls, and evidence of intentional architecture decisions. Code that was generated without human review of each decision leaves a specific fingerprint — and technical auditors in 2026 know exactly what it looks like.

"The LOI was signed at 5x ARR. Technical due diligence took 11 days. The buyer requested a 40% price reduction citing undocumented technical debt. The founder walked. The asset remains unsold."
Deal Watch — H1 2026, Deal 05 — Aegryn Data Desk

This is not an argument against Cursor, Windsurf, or any AI coding tool. It is an argument for applying a documentation and review standard to every piece of code that enters a production codebase — regardless of how it was generated.

Human-reviewed, tested, documented AI-assisted code is indistinguishable from human-written code in due diligence. Unreviewed vibe output is not.

Money — Market Context
The Bifurcation Story.
PHOTO PLACEHOLDER

420 × 130 px

The headline numbers mask a structural shift that matters far more than the volume total. The spread between top-tier and bottom-tier private SaaS valuations has never been wider. Businesses with strong NRR, a clear path to profitability, and a credible AI positioning attract premium valuations. The rest face systematic downward repricing.

The SaaS Capital Index peaked at 16.9x ARR in 2021. It entered 2025 at roughly 7x, then fell to a decade-plus low near 3.2x by mid-2026. It has since recovered modestly to approximately 3.8x ARR as of late July 2026 (Array Capital). The recovery is selective: identity, security, and vertical SaaS are leading. AI-exposed horizontal SaaS is lagging.

"The bifurcation is real. A proper sell-side process with competitive tension is the most reliable way to maximise outcome regardless of where the market sits."

Reading the Map.

Three patterns emerge from this data that are not visible in the headlines. First, AI-adjacent verticals — RegTech, Cybersecurity, AI-native SaaS — have widened their premium gap over horizontal SaaS significantly since 2024. The bifurcation is structural, not cyclical.

Second, the compliance dividend is now a measurable factor in FinTech and LegalTech multiples. Assets that are demonstrably DORA or EU AI Act compliant command 1.5–2x the multiple of equivalent non-compliant assets when EU buyers are in the process. This gap will widen as the AI Act enforcement ramps through 2027.

Third, generic horizontal SaaS is under persistent compression that is not cyclical but structural. AI substitution risk is repricing the category at the buyer level, and no amount of ARR growth is reversing it for undifferentiated products.

Money — Opinion27

This is not an anti-ambition argument. Building something worth €50M requires a genuinely different playbook than building something worth €2M. The point is to know, honestly, which you are building — and to stop applying the wrong playbook to the wrong goal.

The VC multiple required for Scenario A to outperform Scenario B is 5x. How many EU VC funds returned 5x on vintages 2018–2022? Fewer than 12%, per Kauffman Foundation data. That is not an argument against venture capital. It is an argument for knowing which game you are actually playing.

"Clean cap tables, documented IP, transferable contracts, and a product that solves one problem exceptionally well are now exit strategy, not housekeeping."
FE International — Mid-Year 2026

The founders who capture the best outcomes are not the ones who time the market perfectly. They are the ones who are ready when the window opens. Preparation is entirely on the seller's clock. Not the buyer's, not the market's. Yours.

The EU exit ecosystem is mature enough in 2027 to absorb well-prepared assets across all verticals. The question is not whether a serious EU tech asset will find a buyer. It is whether it arrives at the table ready to be found — or whether due diligence finds the problems first.

Two Scenarios.
One Clear Outcome.
Scenario A — Raise €1M at €4M pre-money

You give up 20% equity. Your implicit target: 10x return in 5 years = €40M exit. Probability of achieving that: <5% (Kauffman Foundation, 2025). At a €5M exit — far more common — your net: approximately €4M. You have spent five years chasing a probability that did not materialise.

Net at €5M exit: €4M (80% of €5M)
Scenario B — Bootstrap. Certify. Transact.

No dilution. Reach €400K ARR in 30 months. CIFS certification at Pre-Grade. Transaction at 5x ARR = €2M. Keep 100% of it. Spend 8 months on transition. Then build again — with more capital, more experience, and a certified track record as a seller.

Net: €2M — 100% of it. Repeatable.

Transaction — Buyer Profiles36
Profile 01 — PE Mid-Market Fund
The Platform Builder
Ticket: €3M–€30M equity. Hold period: 4–7 years. Exit: secondary sale or strategic. What they pay for: recurring revenue quality (NRR above 105%), scalable team, documented processes, clean cap table. What they cannot tolerate: founder dependency above F-42 score of 2, revenue concentration above 35% in one client, undocumented IP. Time to close: 8–16 weeks from first meeting. Preference: assets that can be integrated into a portfolio platform within 12 months of acquisition.
Profile 02 — Search Fund
The Individual Operator
Ticket: €500K–€5M, often with institutional backing from search fund investors. Hold period: indefinite — they run it, probably for a decade or more. What they pay for: simplicity, profitability, a founder willing to transition properly. What makes them different: they will pay slightly below PE multiples but offer full retention of the business's identity, no integration pressure, and sometimes an earn-out that benefits a good transition. The fastest-growing acquirer type in EU 2025–2026. Sifted counts at least 18 new search fund launches in Western Europe in H1 2026 alone.
Transaction — Buyer Profiles37
Profile 03 — Strategic Acquirer
The Capability Hunter
Ticket: unlimited in theory, highly selective in practice. Logic: buying capability they cannot build fast enough — an AI layer, geographic access, a customer segment, a regulatory certification. What they pay: a strategic premium of 30–50% above financial buyers when the capability gap is real and verifiable. What they require: a clean integration path, no IP disputes, and a seller willing to stay through the integration period. Timeline: 12–24 weeks average, often longer for cross-border transactions. Key constraint: they have become far more selective since 2023. The mega-checks into broad software categories have largely stopped. They now buy specific, verifiable capability gaps.
Profile 04 — Family Office
The Patient Capital
Ticket: €2M–€20M. Hold period: 10+ years. Logic: cash-generating, defensible assets that don't require active management or aggressive growth. What they pay for: recurring revenue, low churn, founder-independent operations, and a sector they understand. The fastest-growing buyer type for profitable bootstrapped assets in Switzerland, DACH, and Benelux. They don't need a 10x. They need a solid 2x over 10 years — which means they can pay full price without requiring heroic growth assumptions. This alignment is exceptional for founders exiting at €1M–€5M ARR.

Tech & AI — Valuation
The AI Premium Is Real — Here's How to Earn It.

Nearly half of all technology deals in 2025 carried an AI component. But "AI-powered" is no longer a premium signal — it is a baseline expectation. The premium now goes to something specific: a defensible AI architecture that cannot be easily replicated.

When a buyer's technical team evaluates an AI-enabled asset, they are performing one test: is the AI defensible, or substitutable? A feature AI — a ChatGPT wrapper, a standard RAG implementation over public data — is substitutable. A competitor can replicate it in 90 days. A buyer pays no premium for something replaceable.

An architecture AI — proprietary training data with documented rights, custom fine-tuning, a tested model strategy, EU AI Act compliance — is defensible. A competitor cannot replicate it in 90 days because the data doesn't exist elsewhere, the fine-tuning took 18 months of customer feedback, and the compliance framework required a legal and technical investment that most competitors haven't made.

Founders who arrive with a complete AI architecture brief — model provenance, data rights, fallback strategy, compliance status, versioning policy — close AI due diligence 60% faster.
