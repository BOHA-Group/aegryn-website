The AI Premium Is Real — Here's How to Earn It.
Nearly half of all technology deals in 2025 carried an AI component. "AI-powered" is no longer a premium signal — it is a baseline expectation.

When a buyer's technical team evaluates an AI-enabled asset, they perform one test: is the AI defensible, or substitutable? A feature AI — a ChatGPT wrapper, a standard RAG over public data — is substitutable. A competitor can replicate it in 90 days. A buyer pays no premium for something replaceable.

An architecture AI — proprietary training data with documented rights, custom fine-tuning, EU AI Act compliance — is defensible. A competitor cannot replicate it in 90 days because the data doesn't exist elsewhere.

Build — The Preparation Window
The 18-Month Window.
The single most important finding from Aegryn's certification activity: founders who begin preparation 18+ months before a transaction close significantly better than those who start during the process.
Phase 1 — Month −36 to −24
Audit IP ownership. Close contractor agreements. Begin architecture documentation. Commission first pentest. This phase is about eliminating the structural liabilities that buyers will find anyway — better to find them yourself, 24 months before anyone is looking.
Phase 2 — Month −24 to −12
Build ARR verifiability. Reduce F-42 systematically. Pre-Grade CIFS. Engage a fiduciaire for monthly close. Begin reducing founder-critical relationships one account at a time. The goal is not to disappear — it is to make the company demonstrably functional without you in any given meeting.
Phase 3 — Month −12 to −6
Full CIFS Grade. Data room assembly. Buyer universe definition. Advisor selection. This phase is documentation and positioning — not yet a process. Founders who rush from here to a live process skip the most valuable preparation window.
Phase 4 — Month −6 to Close
Process launch. Due diligence from a position of documented strength. Negotiate. Close. The difference between a founder who prepared and one who didn't is not visible in the headline number — it is visible in how much that headline number holds through the process.

Build — Opinion
Vibe Coding Is a Valuation Problem.
The technical auditors find it within 48 hours. What they find — and what it costs founders who built fast but didn't document — is a repeating pattern in EU M&A 2026.
−25%
Typical valuation reduction — vibe-coded core logic found in audit
48h
Time for a technical auditor to identify AI-generated undocumented code

The founder who built 70% of his product with Cursor in six months is not stupid. He is rational. The economics of AI-assisted development are compelling. The problem is not the tooling. The problem is what the tooling leaves behind when the technical auditor opens the repository.

The auditor is not impressed by speed. The auditor is counting: test coverage percentage, documentation density, dependency freshness, and evidence of intentional architecture decisions. Code generated without human review leaves a specific fingerprint — and technical auditors in 2026 know exactly what it looks like.

The fix is not to stop using AI in development. The fix is to treat AI-assisted code the same way you treat any outsourced deliverable: review it, document it, test it, own it.

Transaction — Framework
Anatomy of a Prepared Deal.
The 9 artifacts that separate a professional disposal process from an amateur one. Each artifact has a market price — the delta between having it and not having it.
01 — The Data Room
Not a Google Drive folder. Not a Notion page. A structured repository — legal, financial, technical, operational — that a buyer's team can navigate in 72 hours without a guided tour. Clean indexing, version control, no duplicates, no ambiguity about what is current.
02 — The ARR Schedule
Monthly recurring revenue by customer, by contract, by renewal date, by expansion/contraction history. Presented in a format that a CFO with an Excel addiction can verify in an afternoon. Auditor-signed preferred, CFO-signed minimum.
03 — The CIFS Grade
A third-party analytical grade across five dimensions — not a self-assessment. Buyers use it as a process accelerant. Sellers use it as an anchor in valuation negotiation. Without it, both parties start from zero.
04 — The IP Register
Full list of IP assets: code, brand, patents, domains, data. Ownership documented for each. Contractor agreements attached. Open source licences identified and characterised. GDPR documentation included.
05 — The Technical Architecture Brief
System design, stack components, dependency map, AI architecture (if applicable), security audit summary, infrastructure costs. Written for a CTO who has never seen the product. Not a slide deck — a brief.
06 — The Management Profile
Org chart + individual profiles for all key personnel. F-42 score documented with remediation log. Retention commitments, equity vesting schedule, key person dependencies named and addressed.
07 — The Financial Model
3-year actuals + 3-year model, bottom-up, documented assumptions, unit economics per cohort, payback period, CAC/LTV by channel. Not a hockey-stick slide — a model that holds under buyer scenario analysis.
08 — The Legal Pack
Entity structure, cap table (clean, fully diluted), shareholder agreements, option pool documentation, material contracts, regulatory status. All signatories identified. Nothing outstanding.
09 — The Process Brief
A confidential, 12-page document sent to pre-qualified buyers: summary, context, strategic rationale for acquisition, available information package, process timeline. Written by an advisor. Not by the founder.
